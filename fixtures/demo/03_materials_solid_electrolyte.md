# A Fluorinated Argyrodite Interphase Enables 1,000-Cycle Retention in Sulfide Solid-State Lithium Metal Cells

M. Halvorsen, T. Okada, J. Beaulieu, N. Sarraf — Institute for Energy Storage Materials, Trondheim (preprint, not peer reviewed)

## Abstract

Sulfide solid electrolytes offer high ionic conductivity but degrade rapidly against lithium metal. We report a fluorinated argyrodite interphase, formed in situ from a LiF-rich precursor layer, that suppresses interfacial impedance growth in Li|Li₆PS₅Cl|LiNi₀.₈Mn₀.₁Co₀.₁O₂ cells. Cells retain 88.4% of initial discharge capacity after 1,000 cycles at 0.5C and 60 °C under 5 MPa stack pressure, compared with 41.2% for untreated cells, which fail by short circuit at a median of 340 cycles. Interfacial impedance grows from 12 Ω cm² to 19 Ω cm² over 1,000 cycles in treated cells, versus 12 Ω cm² to 156 Ω cm² in untreated cells. We attribute the improvement to a LiF-rich, electronically insulating interphase that blocks continued electrolyte reduction while remaining ionically permeable.

## 1. Introduction

All-solid-state lithium metal batteries promise higher energy density and improved thermal safety relative to liquid-electrolyte cells. Argyrodite sulfides such as Li₆PS₅Cl reach room-temperature ionic conductivities above 3 mS/cm, which is competitive with liquid electrolytes, but they are thermodynamically unstable against lithium metal. Reduction of the sulfide at the anode interface produces a mixed ionic–electronic interphase that continues to grow, raising impedance and eventually permitting lithium filament penetration.

Interphase engineering strategies have included alloy interlayers, polymer buffers, and halide coatings. Fluoride-rich interphases are attractive because LiF has a wide electrochemical stability window and very low electronic conductivity, but reported LiF interlayers are usually deposited ex situ, which limits conformality on the rough surfaces produced by cold pressing.

## 2. Experimental

### 2.1 Materials and cell assembly

Li₆PS₅Cl was synthesized by ball milling stoichiometric Li₂S, P₂S₅, and LiCl followed by annealing at 550 °C for 5 h under argon. Cathode composite consisted of LiNi₀.₈Mn₀.₁Co₀.₁O₂ (70 wt%), Li₆PS₅Cl (28 wt%), and vapor-grown carbon fiber (2 wt%). Lithium metal foil of 50 μm thickness was used as the anode. All handling was performed in an argon glovebox at H₂O and O₂ below 0.1 ppm.

The fluorinated precursor was applied by dip coating the lithium foil in a 0.1 M solution of a fluorinated ether additive in dimethoxyethane, followed by 12 h of vacuum drying. Interphase formation was completed in situ during three formation cycles at 0.1C.

Cells were assembled as pellet-type cells of 10 mm diameter in a stainless-steel die and cycled under a controlled stack pressure of 5 MPa maintained by a calibrated spring assembly.

### 2.2 Electrochemical measurement

Cycling was performed at 60 °C in a temperature chamber, with charge to 4.3 V at 0.5C, constant-voltage hold to C/20, and discharge to 2.8 V at 0.5C. Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy was measured at 100 kHz to 0.1 Hz at open circuit after every 100 cycles. Interfacial resistance was extracted by fitting to an equivalent circuit with separate bulk and interfacial arcs.

### 2.3 Characterization

Cross sections were prepared by focused ion beam milling under cryogenic conditions and imaged by scanning electron microscopy. Interphase composition was measured by X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy with argon-ion depth profiling. Twelve treated and twelve untreated cells were built for the cycling comparison; three of each were disassembled at 200 cycles for characterization.

## 3. Results

Treated cells retained 88.4% ± 2.1% of initial discharge capacity after 1,000 cycles (n = 9). Untreated cells retained 41.2% ± 8.7% among the cells that survived; six of nine untreated cells failed by soft short circuit before 1,000 cycles, at a median of 340 cycles. First-cycle Coulombic efficiency was 86.9% for treated cells and 84.1% for untreated cells.

Interfacial impedance in treated cells grew from 12 Ω cm² to 19 Ω cm² over 1,000 cycles. Untreated cells grew from 12 Ω cm² to 156 Ω cm² over the same interval, with the steepest growth between cycles 200 and 400.

Depth-profiled X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy of treated cells at 200 cycles showed a fluorine-rich layer approximately 40 nm thick with a LiF-dominated F 1s signature and a strongly attenuated P 2p reduction signal relative to untreated cells. Cryogenic cross sections showed a continuous, conformal interphase in treated cells and a discontinuous, pitted interface with visible lithium penetration at grain boundaries in untreated cells.

Rate capability at 1C and 2C was measured on a separate set of six treated cells; capacity retention after 200 cycles at 1C was 91.7%, and at 2C the cells lost 30% capacity within 60 cycles, which we attribute to lithium depletion at the interface rather than to interphase failure.

## 4. Discussion

The combination of suppressed impedance growth, LiF-dominated interphase composition, and continuous cross-sectional morphology supports the proposed mechanism: an electronically insulating fluoride layer that halts continued sulfide reduction while permitting lithium-ion transport.

The scope of the demonstrated result is narrower than the mechanism. All cycling data are from 10 mm pellet cells at 5 MPa stack pressure and 60 °C. Stack pressure of this magnitude is not representative of pouch-format or automotive-format cells, and pressure is known to suppress void formation at the lithium interface independently of interphase chemistry. Elevated temperature improves both ionic conductivity and interfacial kinetics, and 60 °C results have historically overstated room-temperature durability in this chemistry.

The areal capacity of these cells is 1.6 mAh/cm², which is well below the 4 mAh/cm² typically required for a competitive cell-level energy density. Scaling areal capacity increases the lithium flux through the interphase and is the most likely condition under which the reported retention would degrade.

Finally, the comparison is against untreated cells built in the same batch, which controls for powder synthesis and press conditions but does not compare against other published interphase strategies under matched conditions.

## 5. Conclusion

An in situ fluorinated argyrodite interphase suppresses impedance growth and enables 88.4% capacity retention over 1,000 cycles in pellet-format sulfide solid-state cells at 60 °C and 5 MPa. Demonstrating the same behavior at practical areal capacity, lower stack pressure, and ambient temperature is the remaining requirement for a cell-level claim.
