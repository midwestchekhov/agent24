# Gut Colonization by Lactobacillus reuteri MB-14 Reverses Social Deficits in a Maternal High-Fat-Diet Mouse Model

A. Ruiz-Delgado, Y. Kwon, M. Thibault, R. Ekwueme — Laboratory of Neuroimmune Interactions, Coastal Medical School (preprint, not peer reviewed)

## Abstract

Maternal high-fat diet (MHFD) offspring display reduced sociability in standard three-chamber assays. We show that MHFD offspring have a 12-fold reduction in gut Lactobacillus reuteri relative to controls, and that oral gavage with a single strain, L. reuteri MB-14, restores social interaction time to control levels (sociability index 0.71 ± 0.06 treated versus 0.44 ± 0.08 MHFD vehicle and 0.74 ± 0.05 control, n = 14 per group). Restoration is accompanied by increased oxytocin immunoreactivity in the paraventricular nucleus and is abolished by vagotomy. These results identify a single commensal strain sufficient to reverse a diet-induced social behavior phenotype through a vagus-dependent pathway.

## 1. Introduction

The composition of the gut microbiota has been associated with social, anxiety-like, and repetitive behaviors in rodents, and germ-free animals show altered social preference that is partially reversible by conventionalization. Maternal high-fat diet is one of the more robust dietary manipulations producing an offspring social phenotype, and it also produces a reproducible shift in offspring microbiota composition.

Whether the behavioral phenotype is attributable to a specific taxon or to a broader community shift has been difficult to resolve, because most interventions transfer whole communities. Single-strain reconstitution is the manipulation that most directly addresses sufficiency, though it does not by itself establish necessity.

## 2. Methods

### 2.1 Animals and diet

C57BL/6J females were placed on either a 60% kcal fat diet or a matched control diet for 8 weeks before mating and throughout gestation and lactation. Offspring were weaned at postnatal day 21 onto standard chow. Only male offspring were used for behavioral testing. Litters were culled to 6 pups; no more than 2 pups per litter were assigned to the same experimental group, and litter was included as a random effect in all models.

All procedures were approved by the institutional animal care committee.

### 2.2 Microbiota profiling

Fecal pellets were collected at postnatal day 28 and 56. 16S rRNA V4 amplicon sequencing was performed to a depth of at least 30,000 reads per sample. Shotgun metagenomic sequencing was performed on a subset of 24 samples for strain-level resolution.

### 2.3 Bacterial treatment

L. reuteri MB-14 was isolated from control-diet offspring and cultured anaerobically in MRS broth. Offspring received 2 × 10⁸ CFU by oral gavage every other day from postnatal day 28 through day 56. Vehicle animals received sterile MRS broth on the same schedule.

### 2.4 Behavior

Sociability was measured in a three-chamber assay at postnatal day 60 by an experimenter blinded to group. The sociability index is the fraction of interaction time spent with the novel mouse relative to the total interaction time with the mouse and the object. Open-field and elevated plus maze were run to control for general locomotor and anxiety-like differences.

### 2.5 Vagotomy and histology

Subdiaphragmatic vagotomy or sham surgery was performed at postnatal day 24, with a 4-day recovery before gavage onset. Oxytocin immunoreactivity in the paraventricular nucleus was quantified by cell counts from an average of 6 sections per animal, counted by a blinded rater.

### 2.6 Statistics

Group comparisons used linear mixed models with litter as a random effect, followed by Holm-corrected pairwise contrasts. Sample size of 14 per group was chosen to detect an effect size of d = 1.1 at 80% power, based on the sociability effect reported in prior MHFD work.

## 3. Results

MHFD offspring showed a 12.3-fold reduction in relative abundance of L. reuteri at postnatal day 28 (p < 0.001), confirmed at strain level in the metagenomic subset. Total community diversity was also reduced (Shannon index 3.1 versus 3.8, p = 0.004).

Sociability index was 0.44 ± 0.08 in MHFD vehicle animals versus 0.74 ± 0.05 in control-diet animals (p < 0.001). MHFD animals receiving L. reuteri MB-14 showed a sociability index of 0.71 ± 0.06, not distinguishable from controls (p = 0.61) and higher than MHFD vehicle (p < 0.001).

Open-field distance traveled and elevated plus maze open-arm time did not differ across groups, indicating the sociability difference is not explained by locomotor or general anxiety-like differences.

Oxytocin-immunoreactive cell counts in the paraventricular nucleus were reduced in MHFD vehicle animals (mean 118 versus 176 in controls, p = 0.002) and restored in treated animals (mean 168, p = 0.44 versus control).

In vagotomized animals, L. reuteri MB-14 treatment produced no improvement in sociability (0.46 ± 0.09 versus 0.44 ± 0.08 in vagotomized vehicle, p = 0.83), while sham-operated treated animals improved as before.

Treatment did not restore overall community diversity (Shannon index 3.2 in treated MHFD, p = 0.71 versus MHFD vehicle), indicating the behavioral effect does not require broad community normalization.

## 4. Discussion

A single commensal strain administered orally is sufficient to restore social interaction in this model, and the effect requires an intact vagus nerve. The dissociation between behavioral restoration and unchanged community diversity argues against a nonspecific community-level explanation.

Several boundaries apply. Sufficiency in this model does not establish that L. reuteri depletion is the cause of the MHFD phenotype; a depletion-and-rescue design in the absence of MHFD would be required for that. The vagotomy result shows the pathway is vagus-dependent but does not identify the signal carried, and vagotomy has systemic metabolic consequences that could act through routes other than the intended one.

Only male offspring were tested. Sex differences in both the microbiota response to MHFD and in social behavior assays are well documented, so the result should not be assumed to hold in females without testing.

The three-chamber sociability index is a specific operationalization of social preference in mice, and its relationship to social function in humans is analogical rather than measured. Nothing in this study supports a claim about human neurodevelopmental conditions, and we do not make one.

Finally, oxytocin immunoreactivity is a correlate measured in fixed tissue at a single timepoint. It is consistent with the proposed pathway but is not a causal test of oxytocin's involvement.

## 5. Conclusion

Oral administration of L. reuteri MB-14 restores sociability in male MHFD offspring through a vagus-dependent pathway, without restoring overall microbiota diversity. Extension to female offspring and a depletion-based necessity test are the immediate next steps.
